The Future of Vein Care: Modern Treatments for Varicose & Spider Veins

By the RegenOrtho Palm Beach care team

Vein problems get dismissed as cosmetic more often than almost anything else we treat. Some are. Many are not — the aching by evening, the swelling, the heaviness that makes standing for an hour genuinely unpleasant.

What has changed is what treatment costs you. Vein surgery once meant general anaesthetic and weeks of recovery. Today most vein care is an office appointment and you drive yourself home.

Understanding Vein Disorders

Veins move blood back toward the heart against gravity, using one-way valves to stop it falling back down. When those valves fail, blood pools, and the vein stretches under the pressure.

That produces three related problems: varicose veins, the raised ropey ones that ache; spider veins, fine surface vessels that are usually cosmetic; and chronic venous insufficiency, the long-term version, which brings swelling, skin changes and occasionally ulceration.

Risk runs with family history, age, pregnancy, body weight, and any job that keeps you on your feet all day.

Traditional Vein Treatments (and Their Limitations)

The older options were vein stripping under general anaesthetic, compression stockings, and lifestyle change. Stripping worked, at the cost of a hospital visit and a real recovery. Stockings manage symptoms without addressing the failed valve. Neither was much use to a patient who wanted the problem dealt with and their week back.

Modern Diagnostic Tools

Everything now starts with duplex ultrasound, and it matters more than the treatment choice. Imaging shows which valves have failed, how blood is actually moving, and which vein is driving the problem.

Without it you are treating what is visible on the surface — which is frequently not the vein causing the trouble, and is why some patients find their veins return after treatment elsewhere.

Advanced Minimally Invasive Treatments

Endovenous laser ablation — laser energy seals the failed vein from the inside, under local anaesthetic, through a needle puncture.

Radiofrequency ablation — the same principle using radiofrequency heat. Often covered by insurance where symptoms justify it.

Sclerotherapy — a solution injected into spider and reticular veins, collapsing them so the body clears them over following weeks.

Ambulatory microphlebectomy — surface varicosities removed through punctures that need no stitches. The cosmetic result is immediate.

Benefits of Modern Vein Care

  • Same-day, in-office — no hospital admission
  • Local anaesthetic rather than general
  • Most patients back to normal activity within 24 to 48 hours
  • Better cosmetic outcome than stripping ever produced
  • The ache, swelling and heaviness resolve, not just the appearance

What Patients Can Expect During Treatment

A consultation and ultrasound first, then a plan built on what the imaging showed. The procedure itself is done in the office. Compression stockings for a short period afterwards, and a follow-up scan to confirm the treated vein has closed and stayed closed.

Lifestyle & Prevention Tips

  • Keep moving — walking, cycling and swimming all work the calf pump
  • Break up long periods of standing or sitting
  • Manage weight, which directly changes venous pressure
  • Elevate your legs when you rest
  • Wear compression if it has been recommended

Conclusion

If you have been living with aching, swollen legs on the assumption that treatment means surgery and time off, that assumption is out of date. Come in, let us image the legs properly, and we will tell you what is actually happening — including when the honest answer is that nothing needs doing.
